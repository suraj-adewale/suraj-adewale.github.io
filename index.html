I build computational and statistical methods for human genomics — and I care that they work for the populations genomics has left out.

My doctoral research investigates how polymorphic natural killer cell receptors shape multiple sclerosis. The KIR genes are among the most variable in the genome and their structural complexity has kept them out of reach of genome-wide studies; I wrote PONG 2.0 to recover KIR alleles from the SNP data biobanks already hold.

I trained first as a statistician (B.Sc., M.Sc.) and taught probability and statistics for six years.
